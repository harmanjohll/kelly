Meanings and care.

Singapore is many traditions living side by side, and flowers are read differently in each. These are the customs the consultation respects.

Customs I keep

Chinese

  • Avoid four stems; four (四) sounds like the word for death.
  • White and yellow chrysanthemums, and white arrangements generally, are for mourning.
  • Bright red and pink are joyful colours; they don't belong at a wake.
  • At a wake a standing wreath is customary; a bouquet is better sent to the family home.

Japanese

  • Potted plants are avoided for patients, because 'taking root' suggests a long stay.
  • Camellias are avoided for the sick; chrysanthemums are linked with funerals.

Malay

  • In Malay Muslim custom flowers are less usual at the funeral itself; a simple arrangement for the family home after the burial is a kind gesture.
  • Bunga rampai, a fragrant mix of flowers and pandan, is given to guests at weddings.

Indian

  • Jasmine and marigold are auspicious and joyful.
  • In many Hindu families white leans towards mourning, so happy occasions call for colour.

Families differ, and many blend customs. If you're unsure, tell me in your brief and I'll ask the right questions.

What the flowers say

Rose

Victorian
Love; red for passion, pink for grace and gratitude, white for innocence.
Japanese
Red roses carry romantic love (ai, koi) in hanakotoba.
Chinese
Red is the colour of joy and good fortune, which makes red roses a natural fit for romance and celebration.

Spray rose

Victorian
A smaller cousin of the rose, read as affection and gentleness.

Ranunculus

Victorian
Radiant charm; 'I am dazzled by your charms'.

Chamomile

Victorian
Energy in adversity; patience.

Eucalyptus

Today
A modern florist's green, chosen for its soft scent and silver colour rather than an old meaning.

Chrysanthemum

Chinese
A classical symbol of autumn and long life, but white and yellow chrysanthemums are used for mourning, so they are kept away from celebrations.
Japanese
The imperial flower. White chrysanthemums are strongly linked with funerals.
Victorian
Cheerfulness; white for truth.

Lisianthus

Victorian
Appreciation; a later addition to the flower language, so sources vary.

Lily

Victorian
White lilies for purity and majesty.
Chinese
Bai he (百合) sounds like 'a hundred years together', a wedding wish; white lilies are also common at funerals.
Care
Lily pollen is toxic to cats, and the scent can be strong for patients.

Sunflower

Victorian
Adoration.
Japanese
Adoration and loyalty; 'I only look at you'.

Statice

Victorian
Remembrance; it keeps its colour when dried.

Orchid

Chinese
Lan (兰) stands for refinement and integrity, one of the classical 'four gentlemen' plants.
Singapore
Singapore's national flower is an orchid, the Vanda Miss Joaquim.
Victorian
Refined beauty.

Tulip

Victorian
A declaration of love; yellow once meant hopeless love, now read as cheer.
Japanese
Red tulips for a declaration of love; pink for caring.

Daisy

Victorian
Innocence and loyal love.

Carnation

Today
In Singapore and across East Asia, carnations are the flower of Teachers' Day and Mother's Day.
Japanese
Red carnations for a mother's love.

Jasmine

Indian
Malli or mogra is auspicious: worn in the hair, offered at temples, woven into wedding garlands.
Malay
Melati is loved for its scent and used in bunga rampai, the fragrant mix given at weddings.

Marigold

Indian
Auspicious and joyful; marigold garlands mark weddings, festivals and prayers.

Camellia

Japanese
Tsubaki flowers fall whole from the stem, which some read as an ill omen, so they are avoided for the sick.

The Victorian "language of flowers" was never one fixed code; the old flower dictionaries often disagreed (Beverly Seaton, The Language of Flowers: A History, 1995). Read these as traditions, not rules.

Care

Fleeting

  1. Trim 2cm off each stem at an angle as soon as they arrive.
  2. Use a clean vase and cool water; change it every two days.
  3. Keep away from direct sun, fruit bowls and air conditioning vents.
  4. Remove leaves below the waterline.

Forever

  1. No water, ever.
  2. Keep out of strong sun so the colours stay true.
  3. Dust with a soft, dry brush now and then.
  4. If a petal loses its shape, ask Kelly; crochet can be mended.
